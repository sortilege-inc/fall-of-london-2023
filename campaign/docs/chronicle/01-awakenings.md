---
title: Awakenings
part: Monday, 19 March 2012
---

Tony woke from the toes up. First his fingertips, then the long bones of his arms and legs, stiff
as old rope and slowly giving. Then his throat, which was dry the way a riverbed is dry, and
something running down it. He opened his eyes. A man was bent over him with a goblet, tipping it
a sip at a time.

Lady Catherine felt it before she saw it. Blood, moving again through a body that had gone
decades without, and the body answering like a struck wire. She knew the taste. It was not a
mortal's. It was potent, and it belonged to another of her kind, and for a moment she was
somewhere else with an old lover who had tasted the same way. Then the goblet went away, far too
soon, and she opened her eyes.

She had been honoured to be chosen as a herald. She was still, somewhere under all of it,
sensitive about the northern vowels that money had papered over well enough to open every door
she had wanted opened.

Doctor Banerjee lay where he was and came up out of the thickest fog of the four of them.

Alice came up clearer. Hungry, and still half inside the ritual that had put her down, as though
the words were not quite finished. Someone was murmuring a prayer to Mithras. There were cult
marks cut into the chalice. She could not yet make her mouth work, so she watched the man set the
chalice down.

The four heralds climbed out of their coffins and stood.

"Where are we?" Alice said, once she could. "Who are you? Was the ritual successful?"

"I'll answer everything," the man said. "Shortly."

His name was Adelino da Silva. His job, he said, was to see them fed, clothed and ready to be
briefed, and this was a safe house. There was one thing they needed to understand before
anything else. It was widely believed that Mithras was dead.

"We think it may be otherwise."

---

He took them through to the next room. There were cells along one wall, and people in them. Some
were awake. Some were gagged. Some were not conscious at all.

Da Silva unlocked the doors. "Help yourself."

Alice chose a young grocery clerk. She drank until there was very nearly nothing left, and at the
last moment she stopped.

Tony took the older man. He could feel how frightened the man was, and then, as he fed, he felt
the fear go out of him and something flatter take its place. Resignation.

Doctor Banerjee's was awake, and he did not care for that. He drank what he could anyway.
