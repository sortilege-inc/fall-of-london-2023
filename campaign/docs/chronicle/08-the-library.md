---
title: The Library
part: Thursday, 22 March 2012
---

At Alice's question Sri Sansa looked up, and his face went paler still. Then Alice felt herself
sliding to the floor, and her sight went.

---

Matthias woke to Ayse knocking. He took down his traps and undid his locks. The dead were still on
his mind.

They went straight to the library.

Tony left Ferhat with Darius. Ferhat did not like being left, but Darius had been decent and
reasonable with him, and he trusted Tony a little, for now. Tony went to the library too.

The meeting room was filling. There were chairs around the walls and people standing or sitting on
small rugs and cushions in the middle. The old sat. The young adults clustered and gossiped. Most
of the room was Punjabi, not all of it. There were a couple of *Eat Pray Love* types.

Matthias knew Tony at once, for a Brujah and for a herald. He made a few small movements, and let
Tony know him in return.

The room went quiet. A man in a suit came in, and behind him the guru.

The man in the suit called the meeting to order, gave the agenda, and began to take requests. A
woman came forward and asked about her dead child. Sri Sansa quieted her, and concentrated, and a
faint voice could be heard: a child of eight or nine. Matthias and Tony were both caught by it. It
was nothing like a Hecata rite, and if it was a fake it was a good one.

Then Sri Sansa looked round the room, and his eyes skipped off the two of them. He whispered to the
man in the suit and walked out. The man apologised and offered to take questions himself.

Tony went up. "I have a question for the guru."

The man gave him a card to write it on. Tony wrote down a description of the ring.

---

Alice woke into rain. It was pouring. Ahead of her, on a cobbled street, she caught sight of Sri
Sansa in an old suit jacket, and she followed him.

A little girl, very poor, picked his pocket. Alice went after the girl and stopped her.

"I saw that."

"You saw nothing." The girl held her hands open. Alice could tell there was a coin palmed behind her
fingers.

Alice took a coin out of her own pocket, palmed it the way the girl had, and put it in the girl's
hand.

She remembered being a child herself, in rags, telling strangers' fortunes for a handful of
pennies.

She let the girl go, and followed her a while. This was not the twentieth century. Presently she saw
Sri Sansa again, and the girl picked his pocket again, the same as before, as if the night were
running on a loop.

Alice looked at the girl's aura. Then she looked at everyone's, and they were all the same aura.
That was very strange indeed.

---

While Tony wrote his card, Matthias talked to the woman who had lost her child. She had not seen him
die. She had been told it was a traffic accident. But an agency she did not know had taken the
child away in a black police van, and she had never been given the body.

Matthias began to put it together. If there had been an accident at all, he thought, it was covering
for someone who had made a childe without leave. That was sloppy, and it was stupid.

He kept it to himself. It felt settled, though, and with it settled the dead finally let go of his
thoughts.

Tony came over.

"I believe we are seeking the same person," Matthias said. "Sri Sansa?"

"Yes. Myself and the other heralds."

"Others?"

"There are two others I'm staying with. And a couple of friends."

Matthias looked round. Ayse had gone as well. He had a bad feeling about it, and remembered how
set she had been on getting Sri Sansa's backing for House Carna.

The meetings were weekly, they learned, and there were invitation-only meetings at Sansa's home as
well. They got the address. They decided to go to the safe house first.

On the way Tony told Matthias about Ferhat, and about thin-bloods in general. Tony was a little on
edge. Matthias did not especially care. Neither Tony nor this Ferhat looked like a threat.

Darius was shocked and delighted to find another herald, and barely kept his questions in. He told
Matthias about his book.

---

Lady Catherine came back with news. She had found a penthouse for the heralds.

Oliver was still furnishing it. He let them in and took them away from the windows. What looked like
a wall opened onto a narrow staircase, and at the top of it was a hidden suite: a bunker built for
the night the bombs fall and the flats of London, somehow, stay standing. One bedroom was all sleek
marble with its own bathroom. The other was tiled in mosaic. Beyond them was the living room, where a
twelve-foot river table and tall-backed chairs made it feel like a boardroom. The granite in the
kitchen shone around a big empty island. And the whole room was built for safety: surveillance
systems, the newest there were, and walls of reinforced steel.

---

Alice tried to catch Sri Sansa up. In front of her a carriage crashed. One horse shied and reared
and broke out of its harness, and brought the other down with it, and the carriage went over. The
people inside were bleeding.

The smell was very nearly too much. But she could see that none of it was real, and she held on, and
followed Sri Sansa into a building where a séance was under way.

A woman sat in a trance. Alice kept to the edges of the room. Nobody seemed to know she was there.

She knew some of the faces. Sir Arthur Conan Doyle. The Justicar Juliette Parr. In the corner an old
man who looked exactly like John Dee, and who could not have been.

She looked at the auras again, and again they were all one aura. She began to understand. This was
something that had happened to Sri Sansa. She was inside his memory. Whether it was a trap, or magic,
or something else, she could not tell.

When her eyes came back into focus it was hours later. The séance was over and the room had nearly
emptied. The man who looked like John Dee put a hand on Sri Sansa's shoulder and steered him into
another room. Alice followed and listened at the door.

"Our prince believes that your deep familial connections would be an asset to us. Let me take you to
meet Mithras..."

---

Alice woke with a thick head and a heavy body. She was on a couch. A man and a woman were talking.

The woman wore a hijab and her aura was her own, and the man was Sri Sansa. She was out of the memory.
The woman was arguing that Sri Sansa ought to back House Carna against House Tremere, now that the
Vienna chantry was gone.

Alice understood that the memory had not been something Sri Sansa had done to her, not directly and not
on purpose. She sat up and listened.

"Wonderful, you're awake," said Ayse. "Could you help him understand how important this is?"

Alice said nothing.

"You *are* backing Carna, are you not?"

"I don't think she understands," Sansa said, "that you are a herald."

Alice folded her arms.

"Why did you feel the need to invade my study?" Sansa asked her.

"I was looking for you. You weren't at the library."

"I was at the library tonight, like every Thursday night. I'm not hard to find. You didn't need to
break into my house."

"Where are you going?"

He looked stricken for a moment, and then brightened. "You never liked Bainbridge, did you?"

Alice shrugged. She and Bainbridge had never got on. As Primogen he had technically been above her,
but while Mithras was present her place as Heliodramus had been far above his.

"Well, then you probably won't mind," said Sansa, "that I and some family and followers have found a
lovely village in rural England where we have room and can be safe and secure. I'm sure Mithras, were
he ever to come back, would understand, and would not in the least be inconvenienced by my absence."

"Why does your property not welcome those of Mithras?"

She stood up straight and looked at him. She had been among the most trusted Mithras had. And here was
this *fucking coward*, running away, *warding his house* against Mithras. He might have been a true
believer once. Now, with Mithras supposedly dead, it was plain to her that Sansa was *terrified* of
him, and was running from him and from everything that put him in mind of him.

Sansa had been trusted in the cult. Alice remembered the words in Pater Thomas's letter: *those once
loyal*. She began to suspect everyone.
