---
title: Bunker 788
part: Saturday, 24 March 2012
---

While Adisa, Matthias and Catherine were gone, Tony was binding up his wounds. He noticed something and got
back into the shadows just in time to see a thing like a beast take hold of Alice and drag her away.

He followed, well back. Alice fought it and could not get loose. It came at last to a vault door and turned
the wheel, and Tony caught a glimpse of the room inside before the door shut behind it.

Inside, the creature crossed the room, pulled back a rug, and shifted a steel lid fifteen feet across.
Beneath it was a concrete pit about twenty feet deep. It threw Alice in.

"Wait! I think I know who you are! You don't need to fear me! I have some questions for you!"

The thing said something she could not make out and dragged the lid back over the pit.

Alice's stomach dropped, and the hunger in her got worse.

---

Tony waited in the shadows. The creature came out. It sniffed the air, thought better of it, and went off.
When it was far enough away Tony went into the vault.

It was a war office from the 1940s, built like an auditorium on three levels: desks, desk lamps, filing
cabinets, stacks of paper, rotary telephones, train timetables on the walls decades out of date.

A memory took him. A woman, graceful and set on something, walked up to him and slapped him across the face
so hard she knocked him down. He came up with his fists raised without thinking, and she was gone, and then
she was behind him and pulling him close.

"My sire will be so pleased with you," she said, and she sank her teeth into him.

He came back to the present and started looking round, and heard Alice.

"Anyone hear me?!"

Her voice was muffled, and it was coming from under the floor. It took him ten minutes to find the lid under
the carpet. As he found it he heard the vault door opening again.

---

It was a mithraeum. Torchlight, and ghoul servants. Mithras took his seat. Roger de Camden came after him.
Someone brought in a young man in a stupor. Roger held the young man's wrists over Mithras' chalice and bled
him into it until he died.

"I thank you for joining this ritual," Mithras said. "This mortal has proven worthy of the Blooding, by his
unrelenting service to the cult. We show our gratitude by giving him the gift of the blood."

Catherine could see that de Camden did not believe it would work.

Mithras looked at Catherine and bade her come forward and add her blood. She did. Alice came next, then Tony,
each adding their blood to the rest. Some of it ran over the young man's face as Mithras cradled him. All at
once his eyes flew open and he was thrashing, trying to get away. Mithras crushed his windpipe, and the
desperate look went out of his face. Mithras stood suddenly and let the body fall.

"I appreciate what you have given. We will resume this at a later date. You are dismissed."

---

When the present came back, Catherine was on a lovely antique sofa inside the bunker office. Rea was leaning
on a desk across from her. Beside Rea sat a Nosferatu, his face looking as if it had sloughed away, sharply
dressed in an old suit. The hunger hit Catherine like a punch in the stomach.

Tony and Alice woke in absolute darkness and felt each other there.

"Hello?"

"Is that Tony?"

"Where are you?"

Tony shuffled across until he found Alice's hand on the floor.

"How did you get here?"

He told her what he had seen, and about the room above them.

---

"Richard de Worde, I presume?" said Catherine.

"The one and only. You were looking for me?"

"I'm quite parched. Do you happen to have any sustenance I could partake of?"

"I might. Who did you come with?"

"Well, there was Matthias..."

"Ah, the Puttanesca. I returned him to his family. I owed them a debt, and they can consider it paid."

"...then Alice, and Tony, and Adisa."

"I believe I have two of those three. The Banu Haqim put up something of a fight. Unwisely, shall we say."

"So she is no longer in the world?"

"There is something of her in me."

"I have come prepared to make an exchange."

"You were asking about sustenance." He got up, crossed the room, pulled back the carpet and moved the steel
lid. The light was dim, and it blinded Alice and Tony below. "I believe these are yours."

Catherine looked down. "They are my companions, sir."

Rea threw down a rope ladder, and Alice and Tony climbed out.

"Please," said de Worde. "Take a seat."

Alice looked around, suspicious of everything, and found a chair.

De Worde sat back down. "Rea, bring in the mortal we found with them. Our guests must be famished."

Rea came back with the prisoner from the tunnel. De Worde got up and went behind him.

"The hunger that drives us can be quite distressing."

He bit into the man's neck, and in a few seconds drained him completely. The body shrivelled. There was
nothing left for anyone else. Hunger tore through Tony.

Something was pressing on their minds. Alice felt it. It went into Tony deeper. Catherine saw it for what it
was: games, and a show of power.

De Worde looked round at them and saw what it was doing. "Oh, my apologies. It seems there isn't enough to go
around. Perhaps we'll find something else soon. What brings you here?"

"With respect," said Catherine, "stop with the fucking games and let's get down to business."

"It seems you must be taught respect."

She felt him push at her, nudging her toward the edge. By sheer will she did not go over it. "We came here to
do *business*. I brought something to trade. Your games are uncalled for. Is this how you treat guests? Are you
not civilised enough?"

"I don't have guests. I have victims. I've been keeping an eye on you. You seem to be in league with Mithras,
trying to revive him. Bring him back to this 'life', or whatever you'd call it."

"So you are an enemy of Mithras."

"Enemy implies hostility. This is rational. Can you imagine a vampire a thousand years old ruling the modern
world? I wish to prevent chaos. I wish to avoid the Beckoning. I am here for my own welfare."

"You do not welcome the coming of our Lord?" said Alice. "What have you to fear from him?"

He turned to her. "All these years and still his pup. Still his pet. I've known Mithras for *centuries* longer
than you, Heliodramus."

"You have nothing to fear if you are devoted to him. He loves you as much as he loves the rest of us."

"He loves me as much as he loves you, which is not at all. He has no capacity for love."

"Why do you say that?"

"Do any of us? Love is one of the first things to go."

"Love is a human feeling," said Catherine. "I see what you're saying. But we are his heralds. We have a duty."

"You're looking for his dagger. That, and something to drink. My sorcery has that effect on people."

"I won't lie. I'm parched. But I have something you might want in exchange. I have information on Queen Anne."

"You are decades too late. I don't care what happens in London above."

"You won't be safe down here forever," said Alice.

"It is my only option."

"There's strength in numbers. You could join us."

"It's plain he isn't going to," said Catherine.

"I was born in the eleventh century," said de Worde. "The Beckoning calls to me. I will not answer it. I have
hidden myself away, and it seems I have not done enough to discourage visitors. But I'll make you a bargain. You
leave without the dagger, and I let you slake your thirst."

Alice understood then. The ceremonial dagger was what was feeding the blood sorcery. It was what was driving
their hunger.

"You'll be left down here alone," she said. "I see how the dagger serves you. Is that what you really want?"

"He does have someone," said Catherine, with a nod at Rea. "And I think that monster we saw."

"You met my Guardian. And you know Rea. I'm not alone, and I have my ways of seeing to my needs."

The pressure on their minds came again.

"Alice, let's stop trying to reason with him. Let's count this one as a loss."

"You don't understand. He's using the dagger for his own ends."

"Isn't that what Mithras will do? Isn't it freeing, that Mithras *isn't* here, and we can do as we like?"

"How can you say such a thing?!"

"The old ways are gone. It's a new age. If we bring back someone from the old one, who's to say he won't bring the
roof down on us?"

Alice came up out of her chair. "How *dare* you say such a thing? If I had known this would come out of your
mouth, I would have, I would have..."

De Worde clapped, slowly. "The Ventrue at least understands the situation. And our Tremere understands why I
can't give the dagger to you. Yes, it's somewhere in my kingdom. But the underworld is far too big for you to dig
through all of it. So I wish you the very best of luck in your search, and I bid you adieu."

He vanished in front of them. Rea was gone too.

---

"We'll have to regroup," said Tony. "Shit's piling up."

"Let's get out of here," said Catherine.

Alice began searching the room for the dagger, frantic. She was half starved, and badly shaken.

Catherine opened the vault door to go.

Behind one of the paintings Alice found a safe. Tony came over and got it open, ignoring the hunger in his gut.
Inside were brown paper files, several with their own names on them. It was all from the 1940s: names, contacts,
where each of them fed, what nights they tended to be out. There were photographs. One was of Angelique and
Catherine, at a meeting that was meant to be private. Catherine came over, snatched it, and put it in her bag.

In his own file Tony read that his sire was Saeth, and that Saeth's sire was Gwenllian ferch Arwyn.

Alice handed Tony the papers she had. She had no time for them now. But one sheet caught her eye. It had one line
typed on it.

*The heralds lair lies unprotected. They are weak but not broken.*

---

Alice found a ribbon and began a working with her blood, to light the trail of both de Worde and Rea. She saw
Catherine move to stop her and held up a finger.

"Don't."

"We can't go after them *now*. We're trapped in his lair."

"I need to finish this."

"Fine. I'm leaving. Tony, you can stay or come, but this place is a death trap, and I need to feed."

Tony went with her.

Alice finished the working. The two of them had gone out by a side door that must lead deeper into the lair. It
would hold for one night, she knew. Then she went after the others.

---

Walking, before Alice caught up, Catherine told Tony: "I *am* loyal to Mithras. But sometimes looking disloyal
is the difference between winning and losing."

Tony thought Catherine was tricky, schemes inside schemes, and he could not shake the feeling that this was
another one.

Catherine tried to lead them out. Tony caught her taking a wrong turning and did the directions himself.

---

Out of the station, they split up to feed.

Catherine's Beast did her hunting for her. It took her to a red-light district and gathered up several women
working the street. They were not what she liked, or what she would have chosen, but they took the edge off, and
she went back to the haven a little less desperate.

Tony meant to find a blood bank, or a hospice. Instead he found himself winding through alleys, and a corpse
called to him. It was not a fresh kill and the blood had turned, but it was enough to get him back to the haven.

Alice wanted to feed, but more than that she wanted to be told she was right. A true apostate in Richard de Worde,
and another one possibly in Lady Catherine, had shaken her badly. She wanted the old order of the cult back.

She went back to the occult shop. The same young woman, Laila, was at the counter. Alice fed on her, and then took
her time teaching her the first of the Seven Steps to the Sun: Corax, the Raven. Laila was enthralled with Alice,
and fascinated by the teaching. They agreed a time for Alice to come back.

---

At the haven they found Matthias outside, arguing, or talking, with someone from the Elysium: Calogero Puttanesca.

Catherine saw Alice. "I know you think I'm turning on Mithras. I'm not. I just needed us to get the fuck out of
there." She went inside without waiting for an answer.

Alice showed her the back of her hand, and went in.
