---
title: Wednesday Night
part: Wednesday, 21 March 2012
---

Catherine went hunting on the first night and found a block of expensive flats. A man was
arriving as she did, sharply dressed, talking into a telephone he held against his ear. She caught
his eye.

"Invite me upstairs."

He nodded. She followed him past the security guard and up to the penthouse. Once the door was
shut he ended his call and turned and took her by the wrist. She drew him into what began as a
kiss and ended as a bite.

His name was Oliver Kensington. When she left she promised him she would come back, and that he
would be glad of it. She took his credit card with her.

---

When they woke on Tuesday, Darius was already gone. They kept their heads down that night. On
Wednesday he was still out.

Alice remembered where her chantry had been: the basement of an old parish church in Marylebone.
They walked. Catherine could not get over the clothes in the shop windows, or the traffic, and by
the end of the walk she had begun to read who was who by what they wore. Tony noticed the noise. It
was not loud, but it never stopped, and everyone seemed to carry a little lit thing in the hand.
Alice noticed people talking to no one at all.

The church was kept up, and nearly unused. The chantry's marks had been taken off the outside.
Alice looked for the wards. They were gone too, and not faded: removed. She went round the back,
found a way in, and found nothing. It was a little after half past ten. Someone had scrubbed the
basement of every sign that it had ever been a chantry.

---

They walked the streets around it. At the corner there was a petrol station, and three hard men
giving grief to a woman coming out of the toilet. Close to, the woman's face was a little
masculine.

Alice saw Tony go tight, and saw the Masquerade about to break. She put a hand on his arm and
tried to still his blood with her own. Tony threw it off. He went into a blind rage, and he moved
faster than anything on that forecourt.

In the middle of it he remembered being young, on the docks, found out, and beaten for passing as
a man.

When Tony came back to himself, two of the three were down and did not look alive. He did not seem
troubled by it. He was drinking from the third, and he stopped before the man died.

The woman was getting up, and she was badly frightened, as she had every right to be. Catherine
walked over and caught her eyes. "It's going to be ok. Just look at me. Everything will fade away."

The woman went. Her face stayed with Tony after she had gone.

Alice fed on one of the others. Then they crossed to the shops.

---

It was a small parade: a vintage clothes shop, a jeweller's, and an occult shop that read
fortunes. Tony and Alice went into the occult shop.

The girl behind the counter was, to Alice's eye, exactly the kind of eager young witch Alice had
once been. It was in how she dressed, and in the shape of her aura: someone who went looking for
answers outside the natural order and did not give up. Alice asked about the church. The girl
lit up. She didn't know anything, but there had been strange stories, and then it had all been
explained away as a meth lab in the basement. Some people said it was haunted.

Tony went into the back first for a tarot reading. The Hanged Man, reversed: stagnation; life not
going to plan; action needed to take hold of it again.

"Is there a possibility for this to not end in fire and rubble?"

The next card was the Herald of Pentacles, reversed. Paying the price of neglect. Too little, too
late.

The fortune teller was about thirty, and apologetic. These could be warnings of stagnation, he said,
not fate. Tony found the stagnation darkly funny.

Then it was Alice's turn. "What does the past say about the present?"

He had not drawn a card when it came over her. She was in a room before a god, a being giving off
light, and worshippers all around were bowing to him, and to her in his place. She was in a white
robe. She turned to him and reached out and took the hand of Mithras, and she was his handmaiden,
and his power ran through her.

When she came out of it the fortune teller had not moved. He was stunned. Then he got up out of his
chair and knelt, and put his face to the floor.

"Heliodramus. Handmaiden. By the grace of Mithras it is wonderful to witness you again."

Alice touched his shoulder and asked him to sit. "Where are our brothers and sisters?"

As he drew cards she looked at him hard, and saw what he was. A vampire, technically, and weaker
than any she had ever met. A duskborn. An abomination, and a sign of the end of things.

Three of Swords: rejection. Six of Wands: public recognition, victory, success acknowledged. Both,
he said, were good signs.

He had been taken into the Mithraic mysteries, he told her, and sired by a Tremere. He had lost his
thin ties to the Camarilla court when the Pyramid, the Tremere hierarchy, fell apart after the
chantry in Vienna was destroyed. The whole Tremere council had been killed. The clan had split into
factions at war with one another.

Alice took it very badly.

His own contact, a woman named Ayse, had gone west, and had given them a name: Najma, who could be
found in Southall.

Alice asked about the primogen and the chantry. Edward Bainbridge, he said, was still the Tremere
Primogen.

She scratched a nail across his wrist and flicked a single drop of his blood into her mouth, and
tasted how far he was from Caine. Fourteen steps. She was horrified, and she walked out.

---

Catherine stood at the jeweller's window. She felt a finger on her shoulder, and a face against
her neck, and a scent she knew. Arms went round her. She could not move, and she did not want to.
A pendant was laid against her throat, and a woman whispered that she loved her. The window became
a mirror, and in it Catherine saw her own face and a sapphire at her neck, catching the light from
another room. She turned to see who was behind her. There was only the street.

She put a hand to her neck. There was a necklace there, but it was the one she had taken off the
woman at the warehouse. The one in the vision, she understood, might be gone for good.

She walked into the jeweller's and came out dripping in it, on her banker's card.

Tony went into the vintage shop and came out in a new outfit.

Southall was next.
